# Paracosm FAQ: dice, models, seeds and replay

This page answers what decides a run, what a seed fixes, and what replay, shared links and forks reproduce. Every answer was checked against the code at commit [`063c9ee6`](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/commit/063c9ee6843c43c5285ac7f602f3f5790b4874f2) (npm version 0.9.1095). The answers on who dies (the hazards, the tie and the numbers behind them) follow pull request [#242](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/pull/242), which makes the age and accident rates read the colony's supplies; their links point at commit [`1e6090d3`](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/commit/1e6090d3f210fd86906e48ab005e4b22524839ee). The answers on why the demo leaders chose alike and on what the leader is shown before choosing follow the fix for issue [#248](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/issues/248); their links point at commit [`e18ce8a3`](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/commit/e18ce8a359e9cd11b0d89a6bf98d8d8987590a66), except those on the decision rule's departure line and the check of each reply against the bar, which follow the fix for issue [#250](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/issues/250) and point at commit [`06b4149d`](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/commit/06b4149d0b44bd3719faee70c4443e941bba4b2a). The answer on replay follows the fix for issue [#235](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/issues/235); its links point at commit [`2f1ad727`](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/commit/2f1ad72777fe2ffca5dcabeb30a00565485c3bc9), except those on the server's run-history routes. Each answer that states a mechanism links to the lines that run it.

Words used throughout:

- A **run** is one simulated world played for a number of **turns**. Mars Genesis plays 6 turns of 8 simulated years by default, from 2035, with 100 people ([`scenarios/mars.json`, lines 30 to 36](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/blob/063c9ee6843c43c5285ac7f602f3f5790b4874f2/scenarios/mars.json#L30-L36)).
- The **leader** (the code calls it the commander) decides for the colony. A **pair run** plays two leaders on the same starting world, side by side.
- An **event** is a situation the colony faces in a turn, with two or three options for the leader to choose from.
- **Departments** (medical, engineering, agriculture and others) are model agents. Each event names the departments that look at it, and each of those writes a report on it for the leader; an event that names none of the run's departments goes to the first two ([`commander-setup.ts`, lines 208 to 222](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/blob/063c9ee6843c43c5285ac7f602f3f5790b4874f2/src/runtime/orchestrator/commander-setup.ts#L208-L222)).
- The **kernel** is the part of the engine that holds the numbers (people, food, habitat modules, morale) and changes them by fixed rules.
- **Morale** is the colony's mood, from 0 to 1. Each person's **psych score**, also from 0 to 1, is how well that person is holding up; morale pulls it up or down each year.
- A person's **hazard** for a cause of death is their chance of dying of it in a year, worked out from their state: age, dose, food, psych, bone density, department and, on Mars, the colony's supplies.
- The **seed** is a whole number, 950 for Mars Genesis by default. It starts the **dice**: Mulberry32, a pseudo-random number generator that gives the same sequence of numbers for the same seed every time ([`rng.ts`, lines 1 to 34](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/blob/063c9ee6843c43c5285ac7f602f3f5790b4874f2/src/engine/core/rng.ts#L1-L34)).
- A **language model** (GPT-5.4 or Claude, for example) is called over the network to write text and structured replies. The same request can come back with a different answer.

## What decides what happens in a run, the dice or the model?

Four parts, each with its own job.

1. **Seeded dice decide chance.** Each person rolls one die for each cause of death once for each step of a turn; on Mars Genesis, which keeps one death die for a life, each person keeps one die for each cause for their whole life, read against their chance of having survived it so far. A step is a simulated year; the part of a year a turn ends with is one more step, and a turn of a year or less is one step. Births, partnerships, friendships, recoveries, promotions and lost Earth contacts are rolled the same way, each on a die keyed to one person ([`progression.ts`, lines 495 to 503](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/blob/063c9ee6843c43c5285ac7f602f3f5790b4874f2/src/engine/core/progression.ts#L495-L503)). Each event gets one more draw, which decides whether the option the leader chose succeeds ([`orchestrator/index.ts`, lines 1600 to 1607](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/blob/063c9ee6843c43c5285ac7f602f3f5790b4874f2/src/runtime/orchestrator/index.ts#L1600-L1607)). The seed also builds the starting roster.
2. **Formulas set each chance and each change.** Ageing, the radiation dose, bone density, morale and psych, food, and each cause's yearly hazard are fixed formulas with no dice in them ([`progression.ts`, lines 274 to 327](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/blob/063c9ee6843c43c5285ac7f602f3f5790b4874f2/src/engine/core/progression.ts#L274-L327); [`mars-radiation-bone.ts`](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/blob/063c9ee6843c43c5285ac7f602f3f5790b4874f2/src/engine/physics/mars-radiation-bone.ts#L55-L94)). Personality drift is a formula too: it reads the leader's traits, the person's department and the event's outcome ([`progression.ts`, lines 74 to 117](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/blob/063c9ee6843c43c5285ac7f602f3f5790b4874f2/src/engine/core/progression.ts#L74-L117)).
3. **The scenario's tables say what an outcome does.** For each event category, the scenario lists what an ordinary response brings: on Mars an infrastructure event brings 2 habitat modules, 60 kW of power and 200 m³ of pressurized volume ([`scenarios/mars.json`, lines 177 to 212](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/blob/063c9ee6843c43c5285ac7f602f3f5790b4874f2/scenarios/mars.json#L177-L212)). The outcome scales each gain in the table: 2.5 times for a risky success, 1 for a safe success, -1 for a safe failure and -2 for a risky failure ([`effects.ts`, lines 6 to 11](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/blob/063c9ee6843c43c5285ac7f602f3f5790b4874f2/src/engine/registries/effects.ts#L6-L11)). A cost in a table, an entry that leaves the colony worse off, works the other way: nothing on a risky success, 1 time on a safe success, 3 times on a safe failure and 4 times on a risky failure ([`effects.ts`, lines 47 to 65](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/blob/063c9ee6843c43c5285ac7f602f3f5790b4874f2/src/engine/registries/effects.ts#L47-L65)). Mars's tables hold gains only. The scenario also fixes the starting values and, on Mars, the events of the first and last turns.
4. **The language model writes.** It writes the events of the turns between the first and the last, each department's report, the leader's choice of option and of the effects the departments propose, the department heads named before turn 1, the colonists' reactions, and the verdict that compares two leaders.

So a run is more than dice. The dice decide luck against chances the formulas set. The model's choices change the state those formulas read, within limits the next answers describe.

## Do dice pick the events?

No. On Mars the first and last turns play events the scenario fixes: "Landfall" and "Legacy Assessment" ([`scenarios/mars.json`, line 1113](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/blob/063c9ee6843c43c5285ac7f602f3f5790b4874f2/scenarios/mars.json#L1113); [`orchestrator/index.ts`, lines 1040 to 1058](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/blob/063c9ee6843c43c5285ac7f602f3f5790b4874f2/src/runtime/orchestrator/index.ts#L1040-L1058)). For the turns between, a model called the Event Director writes each leader's events from that leader's state, one or two a turn on Mars ([`orchestrator/index.ts`, lines 1059 to 1120](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/blob/063c9ee6843c43c5285ac7f602f3f5790b4874f2/src/runtime/orchestrator/index.ts#L1059-L1120)). It writes the situation, two or three options, which option is risky, the risky option's chance of success, the event's category and the departments that report on it.

The dice come in after the leader chooses. The engine takes the risky option's chance, adds 0.1 when morale is above 0.7 and 0.05 when the colony holds more than 12 months of food, takes off 0.05 when more than 150 people live, and holds the result between 0.1 and 0.9. A safe option halves the chance of failure: its chance is `(1 + risky) / 2` ([`progression.ts`, lines 150 to 173](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/blob/063c9ee6843c43c5285ac7f602f3f5790b4874f2/src/engine/core/progression.ts#L150-L173)). One draw, keyed by the seed, the turn and the event's place in the turn, meets that chance. Both leaders of a pair get the same draw for the same event slot, so on one draw a risky choice can fail where a safe one succeeds ([`progression.ts`, lines 175 to 184](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/blob/063c9ee6843c43c5285ac7f602f3f5790b4874f2/src/engine/core/progression.ts#L175-L184)).

The outcome's multiplier then scales the category's table, with three small terms added to it: one from the leader's openness and conscientiousness, a seeded noise term between -0.1 and 0.1 that both leaders of a pair share for the same event slot, and one from the departments' tool forging in that event ([`orchestrator/index.ts`, lines 1609 to 1650](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/blob/063c9ee6843c43c5285ac7f602f3f5790b4874f2/src/runtime/orchestrator/index.ts#L1609-L1650)).

The leader sees these numbers before choosing. Its decision prompt states the risky option's chance and the safe options' chance, computed this way from the state at that moment, and what a gain and a cost are multiplied by on each outcome ([`commander-setup.ts`, lines 236 to 264](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/blob/e18ce8a359e9cd11b0d89a6bf98d8d8987590a66/src/runtime/orchestrator/commander-setup.ts#L236-L264)). The draw meets the chance after the effects the leader adopts are applied, so the two differ only when an adopted effect carries morale across 0.7, food across 12 months or the population across 150.

## What does the seed control?

The seed fixes everything the kernel draws:

- **The starting roster**, for the same population and key personnel: names, ages, traits and departments ([`kernel.ts`, line 228](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/blob/063c9ee6843c43c5285ac7f602f3f5790b4874f2/src/engine/core/kernel.ts#L228)).
- **Each person's dice**: deaths, births, partnerships, friendships, recoveries, promotions and lost Earth contacts. Each roll has a stream of its own, keyed by the seed, the turn, the step within the turn, the person and what is rolled for (`SeededRng.forDraw`, [`rng.ts`, lines 51 to 66](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/blob/063c9ee6843c43c5285ac7f602f3f5790b4874f2/src/engine/core/rng.ts#L51-L66)). So one person's fate never moves another person's dice, and two runs on one seed roll the same dice for a person both rosters hold under the same id. A scenario that keeps one death die for a life (`setup.mortalityDice: "life"`; Mars Genesis does) keys each person's death dice by the seed, the person and the cause alone, not by the turn or the step: one die for each cause for as long as the person lives (`SeededRng.forLife`, [`rng.ts`, lines 68 to 76](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/blob/2423aeac13a393df014ca07d914cecaa22c01074/src/engine/core/rng.ts#L68-L76)).
- **Each event's outcome draw and its noise term** ([`orchestrator/index.ts`, lines 1601 and 1609](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/blob/063c9ee6843c43c5285ac7f602f3f5790b4874f2/src/runtime/orchestrator/index.ts#L1601-L1609)).
- **A newborn's name, id and traits**, which come from the turn's shared stream ([`progression.ts`, lines 692 to 708](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/blob/063c9ee6843c43c5285ac7f602f3f5790b4874f2/src/engine/core/progression.ts#L692-L708)), and who joins or leaves when an effect changes the head count ([`kernel.ts`, lines 682 to 696](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/blob/063c9ee6843c43c5285ac7f602f3f5790b4874f2/src/engine/core/kernel.ts#L682-L696)).

The same die is not the same fate. A die kills a person when it falls under that person's hazard, and the hazard reads that run's state: age, dose, food, psych, bone density, department and, on Mars, the colony's supplies. With a die for life, the die kills the person in the year their chance of having survived the cause, which each year's hazard lowers, falls to it.

The seed does not control anything the model writes: the Event Director's events, the department reports, the leader's choices, the department heads, the reactions or the verdict. A rerun of the same seed makes new model calls, and they can come back different. Once one choice differs, the states differ, and the same dice can land differently.

## What stops the model from making things up?

The model can write any words. What its words can change in the numbers is held by these rules:

- **Every structured reply is checked against a schema.** The Event Director's events, each department report, each decision, the reactions and the verdict must parse as JSON of a fixed shape. A reply that fails is asked for again, up to 2 more times by default ([`sendAndValidate.ts`, lines 91 to 161](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/blob/063c9ee6843c43c5285ac7f602f3f5790b4874f2/src/llm/sendAndValidate.ts#L91-L161) for the reports and decisions; [`generateValidatedObject.ts`](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/blob/063c9ee6843c43c5285ac7f602f3f5790b4874f2/src/llm/generateValidatedObject.ts#L80-L125) for the rest). After that the run uses a fallback: an empty department report, a decision that defers to the departments (which reads as the safe option), one of three prepared events, or no verdict ([`director.ts`, lines 242 and 359 to 361](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/blob/063c9ee6843c43c5285ac7f602f3f5790b4874f2/src/runtime/orchestrator/director.ts#L359-L361)). An event must mark its risky option as risky ([`validators/director.ts`, lines 22 to 35](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/blob/063c9ee6843c43c5285ac7f602f3f5790b4874f2/src/runtime/validators/director.ts#L22-L35)).
- **Effects move only keys the run holds.** A department may propose effects, such as "+1 habitat module, -35 kW". The schema refuses an effect that names a key the run does not hold as a number and lists the keys it does hold ([`validators/department.ts`, lines 78 to 104](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/blob/063c9ee6843c43c5285ac7f602f3f5790b4874f2/src/runtime/validators/department.ts#L78-L104)). A report that names one after its retries is kept without those keys (the salvage, [lines 106 to 143](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/blob/063c9ee6843c43c5285ac7f602f3f5790b4874f2/src/runtime/validators/department.ts#L106-L143)). The kernel drops any key it does not hold in any case ([`kernel.ts`, line 650](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/blob/063c9ee6843c43c5285ac7f602f3f5790b4874f2/src/engine/core/kernel.ts#L650)).
- **One decision moves a key a bounded amount.** The effects a leader adopts move each key at most the largest entry the scenario's tables give it: on Mars 2 habitat modules, 4 months of food, 0.15 of morale and 60 kW of power. A key no table names moves at most a tenth of its value, or 1 when the value is 0 ([`commander-policy.ts`, lines 51 to 98](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/blob/063c9ee6843c43c5285ac7f602f3f5790b4874f2/src/runtime/orchestrator/commander-policy.ts#L51-L98)).
- **Department patches set nothing.** A report's `proposedPatches` (values written outright) are logged and ignored; only adopted effects move the state ([`util/parsers.ts`, lines 188 to 242](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/blob/063c9ee6843c43c5285ac7f602f3f5790b4874f2/src/runtime/util/parsers.ts#L188-L242)).
- **A report's note on a person is bounded.** It moves that person's psych score at most 0.15 a turn and sets their list of conditions. It cannot set a dose, a bone density, or who is alive ([`kernel.ts`, lines 312 to 329](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/blob/063c9ee6843c43c5285ac7f602f3f5790b4874f2/src/engine/core/kernel.ts#L312-L329) for the bound per turn, [lines 775 to 807](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/blob/063c9ee6843c43c5285ac7f602f3f5790b4874f2/src/engine/core/kernel.ts#L775-L807) for the note).
- **Only the dice kill.** Every death comes from the kernel's rolls between turns ([`progression.ts`, lines 531 to 607](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/blob/063c9ee6843c43c5285ac7f602f3f5790b4874f2/src/engine/core/progression.ts#L531-L607)).
- **Forged tools run sandboxed and judged.** A department can write a small JavaScript tool to compute something. It runs in an isolated JavaScript context (`node:vm`) with a 10-second limit, and a judge model approves it only when it passes both a safety and a correctness review of its code and test output ([`emergent-setup.ts`, lines 210 to 237](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/blob/063c9ee6843c43c5285ac7f602f3f5790b4874f2/src/runtime/orchestrator/emergent-setup.ts#L210-L237)). A tool's output is text in the report. What reaches the numbers is the count of tools forged, approved and rejected that event, which shifts the outcome's scale by at most 0.5 and costs power and morale ([`effects.ts`, lines 115 to 166](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/blob/063c9ee6843c43c5285ac7f602f3f5790b4874f2/src/engine/registries/effects.ts#L115-L166)).

What stays model writing with no check:

- **The event's text and its numbers within range.** The Event Director picks the risky option's chance (the engine holds it between 0.1 and 0.9) and the category. A category the scenario has no table for moves morale alone, by 0.08 at an ordinary response ([`effects.ts`, lines 73 and 102 to 104](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/blob/063c9ee6843c43c5285ac7f602f3f5790b4874f2/src/engine/registries/effects.ts#L102-L104)).
- **The size of a proposed effect, within its bound.** An adopted effect costs nothing of its own unless the department wrote a cost into it.
- **The report prose**: summaries, risks and recommendations.
- **Citations.** The schema asks each citation for a text and a URL and nothing more ([`validators/department.ts`, lines 14 to 19](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/blob/063c9ee6843c43c5285ac7f602f3f5790b4874f2/src/runtime/validators/department.ts#L14-L19)). The code does not open the URL or check that it says what the report says. When a report gives no citation and the event's research packet holds sources, the run attaches up to five of the packet's facts as the report's citations ([`orchestrator/index.ts`, lines 1259 to 1271](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/blob/063c9ee6843c43c5285ac7f602f3f5790b4874f2/src/runtime/orchestrator/index.ts#L1259-L1271)). The packet comes from the scenario's knowledge bundle, or from a web search when live search is on.
- **The reactions.** Colonists' quotes change no number the kernel holds. The step after them writes each quote into the person's memory and moves their stances and relationships, but it writes into copies of the state that the kernel does not keep, so none of it reaches the next turn or the saved run ([`reaction-step.ts`, lines 189 and 213 to 226](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/blob/063c9ee6843c43c5285ac7f602f3f5790b4874f2/src/runtime/orchestrator/reaction-step.ts#L189-L226); issue [#241](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/issues/241)).
- **The verdict.** The prompt lists both colonies' final numbers side by side. The scores, the winner and the reasoning are the model's, and no code checks them against the numbers ([`pair-runner.ts`, lines 789 to 834](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/blob/063c9ee6843c43c5285ac7f602f3f5790b4874f2/src/cli/pair-runner.ts#L789-L834)). The verdict card's own figures come from the engine.

## What does replay do?

Replay (`wm.replay(artifact)`, or `POST /api/v1/runs/:runId/replay` on the server) takes a saved run that holds at least one recorded decision and three sets of kernel snapshots, which the turn loop saves: the state the run's first between-turn step starts from (`kernelSnapshotAtStart`), the state right after each turn's between-turn step, before the turn's events (`kernelSnapshotsAfterProgression`), and the state at the end of each turn (`kernelSnapshotsPerTurn`) ([`orchestrator/index.ts`, lines 1153 to 1160](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/blob/2f1ad72777fe2ffca5dcabeb30a00565485c3bc9/src/runtime/orchestrator/index.ts#L1153-L1160) and [lines 1864 to 1869](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/blob/2f1ad72777fe2ffca5dcabeb30a00565485c3bc9/src/runtime/orchestrator/index.ts#L1864-L1869)). The dashboard saves them for every run it starts. A run started from code saves them only when its options set `captureSnapshots: true`: the default for `runSimulation` and `wm.simulate` is false ([`orchestrator/index.ts`, lines 397 to 408](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/blob/2f1ad72777fe2ffca5dcabeb30a00565485c3bc9/src/runtime/orchestrator/index.ts#L397-L408)), and `wm.quickstart` sets it to true unless told otherwise ([`world-model/index.ts`, line 490](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/blob/2f1ad72777fe2ffca5dcabeb30a00565485c3bc9/src/runtime/world-model/index.ts#L490)). On the server, the replay route is one of the `/api/v1/` run-history routes, which answer 403 with `run_history_routes_disabled` in hosted-demo mode (`PARACOSM_HOSTED_DEMO=true`) unless `PARACOSM_ENABLE_RUN_HISTORY_ROUTES=true` turns them on, and in any mode when that variable is `false` ([`server-app.ts`, lines 1369 to 1374](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/blob/063c9ee6843c43c5285ac7f602f3f5790b4874f2/src/server/server-app.ts#L1369-L1374); [`routes/platform-api.ts`, lines 63 to 73](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/blob/063c9ee6843c43c5285ac7f602f3f5790b4874f2/src/server/routes/platform-api.ts#L63-L73); [HTTP_API.md](HTTP_API.md)).

For each turn, replay restores the kernel from the state that turn's step started from (the start snapshot for the first, else the end-of-turn snapshot of the turn before), runs the between-turn step to the turn's saved turn and time, and compares the fresh state with the one the run saved right after that step, field by field (as canonical JSON). Nothing writes the kernel between the end of one turn and the next step, so the state each step starts from is the one the run had. Replay reports `matches` and, when they differ, the first path that differs, from the artifact's root, with the saved and the fresh value, in the form `/scenarioExtensions/kernelSnapshotsAfterProgression/1/state/metrics/morale (<saved> vs <fresh>)` ([`orchestrator/index.ts`, lines 2222 to 2279](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/blob/2f1ad72777fe2ffca5dcabeb30a00565485c3bc9/src/runtime/orchestrator/index.ts#L2222-L2279); [`world-model/index.ts`, lines 617 to 658](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/blob/2f1ad72777fe2ffca5dcabeb30a00565485c3bc9/src/runtime/world-model/index.ts#L617-L658)). The between-turn step is the kernel's yearly work: ageing, the dose, deaths, births, partnerships, promotions, food and morale. Replay calls no model, so it costs nothing.

What a match proves: the between-turn step, run on today's code, turns the state each checked turn started from into the state the run saved after it. That is the kernel's half of a run, checked for that artifact. The tests replay a two-turn Mars Genesis run through the real turn loop, its decisions the replies Aria Chen wrote in the hosted pair of 11 November 2026 given by a stub model, to `matches: true` ([`replay-turn-loop.test.ts`, lines 50 to 76](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/blob/2f1ad72777fe2ffca5dcabeb30a00565485c3bc9/tests/runtime/world-model/replay-turn-loop.test.ts#L50-L76)).

## What does a shared link do?

A link of the form `paracosm.agentos.sh/sim?replay=<sessionId>` plays back a stored run. It does not run anything again.

The dashboard reads the `replay` parameter ([`App.tsx`, lines 145 to 164](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/blob/063c9ee6843c43c5285ac7f602f3f5790b4874f2/src/dashboard/src/App.tsx#L145-L164)), checks that the session exists with `GET /sessions/<id>`, and opens `/sessions/<id>/replay` ([`useSSE.ts`, lines 505 to 538](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/blob/063c9ee6843c43c5285ac7f602f3f5790b4874f2/src/dashboard/src/hooks/useSSE.ts#L505-L538)). That endpoint streams back the events the live run broadcast, word for word: at once by default, or at the recorded pace with `?speed=1` ([`server-app.ts`, lines 2753 to 2809](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/blob/063c9ee6843c43c5285ac7f602f3f5790b4874f2/src/server/server-app.ts#L2753-L2809)). It runs no kernel and calls no model, so every viewer sees the same run and it costs nothing.

The server saves a finished run on its own, unless the run was aborted, hit an error or stopped short of its turns ([`server-app.ts`, lines 1140 to 1260](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/blob/063c9ee6843c43c5285ac7f602f3f5790b4874f2/src/server/server-app.ts#L1140-L1260)). It keeps the 10 newest saved runs and deletes the oldest past that ([`stores/session.ts`, lines 29 and 287 to 297](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/blob/063c9ee6843c43c5285ac7f602f3f5790b4874f2/src/server/stores/session.ts#L287-L297)). A link to a deleted run shows a "not found" banner.

A link with `?load=<url>` is a different thing: it loads a run saved as a JSON file from that address.

## What does a fork reuse, and what does it generate fresh?

A fork starts a new branch from the end of one turn of a saved run, with one leader, often a different one. The dashboard's Reports tab has a fork button on each turn; the library call is `wm.forkFromArtifact(artifact, atTurn)` ([`world-model/index.ts`, lines 580 to 614](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/blob/063c9ee6843c43c5285ac7f602f3f5790b4874f2/src/runtime/world-model/index.ts#L580-L614)).

Reused from the trunk, without rerunning it: the kernel snapshot at the end of that turn. That is every person (age, health, dose, bone density, psych, department, partner), the colony's numbers, the event log, the seed the dice are keyed by, and the kernel's own stream ([`kernel.ts`, lines 369 to 414](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/blob/063c9ee6843c43c5285ac7f602f3f5790b4874f2/src/engine/core/kernel.ts#L369-L414)). The fork runs from the next turn on ([`orchestrator/index.ts`, lines 655 to 675](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/blob/063c9ee6843c43c5285ac7f602f3f5790b4874f2/src/runtime/orchestrator/index.ts#L655-L675)).

Generated fresh, with model calls: every turn after the fork. That means the Event Director's events (on Mars the last turn's event stays the scenario's), the department reports, the new leader's choices and the reactions. A department whose head is alive keeps them; the new leader names a head only for an empty seat ([`commander-setup.ts`, lines 281 to 324](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/blob/063c9ee6843c43c5285ac7f602f3f5790b4874f2/src/runtime/orchestrator/commander-setup.ts#L281-L324)). The toolbox of forged tools, the departments' memory of earlier turns and the Event Director's list of earlier events start empty ([`orchestrator/index.ts`, lines 728, 940 and 967](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/blob/063c9ee6843c43c5285ac7f602f3f5790b4874f2/src/runtime/orchestrator/index.ts#L728)). A fork gets no verdict: the Branches tab compares it with its parent.

The seed on a fork: each person's dice stay keyed by the trunk's seed, which the snapshot carries. The fork dialog's seed (the trunk's, unless you set an override) keys the events' outcome draws and noise ([`ReportView.tsx`, line 241](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/blob/063c9ee6843c43c5285ac7f602f3f5790b4874f2/src/dashboard/src/components/reports/ReportView.tsx#L241)). An override changes the luck of the events and leaves the people's dice as they were.

An intervention, here, is a custom event you add in the fork dialog, as `turn: title: description`. The run appends it as a "USER EVENT" line to the text of that turn's events, for the models to read ([`runtime-helpers.ts`, lines 46 to 68](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/blob/063c9ee6843c43c5285ac7f602f3f5790b4874f2/src/runtime/util/runtime-helpers.ts#L46-L68)). The kernel does not read it: it moves a number only through what the leader then chooses. The Digital Twin's interventions are a separate feature.

## Why can two leaders on one seed lose the same people?

Because both colonies start with the same people, and each person the two colonies began with rolls the same dice under both leaders while they stay on both rosters, so that person's fate differs only where the two colonies give them different chances. In colonies alike in department heads, food and psych, those chances differ only through the supplies and the dose, and a small difference between the colonies moves them a little.

With a die a year (every scenario but Mars Genesis), a die kills a person when it falls under that person's hazard for that year, and a person dies under one leader and lives under the other only when one of their yearly dice falls between the two colonies' hazards for them in that year; a die seldom falls between two chances that close. With a die for life (Mars Genesis, since [issue #243](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/issues/243)), every year's chance counts toward one number, the person's chance of having survived the cause so far, so two colonies that give a person different chances record that person's death in different years wherever that number falls to their die in a different year. The person dies under one leader only where their die lies between the two colonies' chances of having survived that cause to the run's last year. The yearly hazards and what each reads ([`progression.ts`, lines 254 to 370](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/blob/1e6090d3f210fd86906e48ab005e4b22524839ee/src/engine/core/progression.ts#L254-L370)):

|---|---|---|
| Natural | age 60 and over; the rate divides by the colony's supply level | the supply level: life-support capacity, water, power, pressurized volume and habitat modules against what the colony landed with, which outcomes and adopted effects move |
| Accident | age 18 and over; the rate depends on the department and divides by the supply level | which department a person heads, through the leader's choice of heads, and the supply level, as for natural deaths |
| Starvation | under 1 month of food | food; the live Mars pair below held 19 to 74 months at its turn ends |
| Despair | psych under 0.2, age 18 and over | psych, through morale; in the live pair below morale sat at 1, its ceiling, from turn 3 on |
| Fatal fracture | bone density under 60 percent, age 40 and over | nothing on Mars: bone density stops at 90 percent of a person's start, 94 for the Mars-born |
| Radiation cancer | a dose above 0.5 Sv, age 30 and over | the dose, through habitat modules |

## Why did the two demo leaders choose alike, and what makes them differ?

In three live Mars Genesis pairs on the hosted demo (8 November 2026 at 06:06 UTC, 10 November at 02:32 UTC and 10 November at 14:06 UTC; seed 950, Aria Chen against Dietrich Voss, 6 turns of 8 years, 30 people, 3 departments), the two leaders made 41 decisions and chose a safe option in 40 ([issue #248](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/issues/248)). Aria Chen ("The Visionary") took the bold option, the one the event marks risky, in none of her 19 decisions; Dietrich Voss ("The Engineer") took it in 1 of his 22. On the two events every Mars pair shares, "Landfall" and "Legacy Assessment", they chose the same option in all 6 slots.

The model decides from what its prompt says, and the two leaders' prompts said nearly the same:

1. The leader's profile reached a decision prompt through one line about drift and nothing else. The decision style went out once, before turn 1, and its cues fired only strictly above 0.7 or strictly below 0.3, so the presets' traits written at 0.30 and 0.70 were not heard.
2. The departments write their reports from the event's text and the state, without the options, and the prompt asked whether the reports converged. All 41 recorded reasons said they did. The one bold choice came on the one event where a report asked for the bold action.
3. The bold option came marked `[RISKY]` with no chance and no payoff. At those runs' states its chance was 57 to 80 in 100, and on the average multiplier it was the better choice in 37 of the 41 decisions.
4. Mars Genesis's own director instructions replaced the default ones, and only the default held the rules on writing a real trade-off. In the 29 events the director wrote, the bold option sat at `option_b` every time and read as skipping a safety step ("bypass" in 15 labels).
5. A leader's drift capped the outcome pull at 0.05 and then multiplied it by the turn's length, so one safe success on a Mars turn moved conscientiousness by 0.16. By turn 6 both leaders stood at 0.95 in conscientiousness, agreeableness and honesty-humility in all three pairs, and from turn 4 the drift line told the Visionary so.

The three pairs ran one model for the commander and the director, so they do not show how much of the caution is that model's own.

The fix for [issue #248](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/issues/248) changes what the model reads. The model still chooses.

- **Every decision prompt carries a decision rule** ([`commander-setup.ts`, lines 286 to 316](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/blob/06b4149d0b44bd3719faee70c4443e941bba4b2a/src/runtime/orchestrator/commander-setup.ts#L286-L316); [`orchestrator/index.ts`, lines 1567 to 1590](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/blob/06b4149d0b44bd3719faee70c4443e941bba4b2a/src/runtime/orchestrator/index.ts#L1567-L1590)). It states the bold option's chance and the safe options' chance, as the outcome step computes them; what the event gains is multiplied by (2.5, -2, 1 and -1) and what it costs is multiplied by (0, 4, 1 and 3) on a bold success, a bold failure, a safe success and a safe failure; and the leader's bar, the chance at or above which this leader takes the bold option. It says the reports were written without the options, and that the chances and multiples already include the dangers the event describes. It asks the commander to choose by its bar, and to depart from it only for a fact these odds do not count, written with its number in the reply's own `barDeparture` field. It states odds and a bar and does not pick the option.
- **The bar comes from the profile as it stands that turn** ([`commander-setup.ts`, lines 123 to 189](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/blob/e18ce8a359e9cd11b0d89a6bf98d8d8987590a66/src/runtime/orchestrator/commander-setup.ts#L123-L189)). The appetite is `(openness + (1 - conscientiousness) + (1 - emotionality)) / 3`, and the bar is `4/7 + 0.8 * (0.5 - appetite)`, held within 0.1 and 0.9. ## What does a run cost, and what is free?

Free, because no model is called: replay, and a shared `?replay=` link.

Paid, because they call the model: a new run, a rerun of a seed, and a fork, which calls the model for every turn after the fork point. The fork dialog shows an estimate before you confirm.

Measured on the hosted demo: the Mars pair of 10 November 2026 (6 turns, 30 people, 3 departments, GPT-5.4 mini for the departments and the tool judge, GPT-5.4 nano for the rest) cost $0.6243 in 58 model calls for Aria Chen and $0.8191 in 65 calls for Dietrich Voss, $1.44 for the pair ([issue #234](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/issues/234)). The Mars pairs of 11 November 2026 (the same setup, the commander on GPT-5.4 nano) cost $0.6420 and $0.6380, of which the commander $0.0292 and $0.0265; the same pair with the commander on GPT-5.4 mini cost $0.7043, of which the commander $0.0889 ([issue #252](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/issues/252)). The verdict is one more call, on GPT-5.4 mini, outside those two figures ([`pair-runner.ts`, lines 195 to 216](https://github.com/framerslab/paracosm/blob/063c9ee6843c43c5285ac7f602f3f5790b4874f2/src/cli/pair-runner.ts#L195-L216)). ## Does the same seed give the same run?

It gives the same start and the same dice. The run itself can differ.

Rerun a seed with the same scenario, setup (population, key personnel, start time, number of turns and time per turn) and leaders, and the roster starts the same, each person rolls the same dice, each event slot gets the same outcome draw, and Mars's first and last events are the same. Everything the model writes comes from new calls: the events of the middle turns, the reports, the choices, the reactions and the verdict. Those can differ, and once one choice differs, the colony's state differs, and the same dice can land on different fates. On Mars Genesis, which keeps one death die for a life, a rerun whose model answers differ can change when people die as well as who: a different chance in any year can move the year a person's die comes due.

To see the same run again, open the stored run: its `?replay=` link, or its saved JSON file. To check the kernel's half of a saved run against today's code, use replay; a run saved before the turn loop kept the snapshots replay compares gets the answer that replay cannot verify it ([What does replay do?](#what-does-replay-do)).
